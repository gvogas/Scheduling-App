ES Pro Accessibility

Scheduling app for managing appointments, clients, and employees · by Plombier Eau Secours
ES Pro for iPhone, version 1.61 · Last updated September 14, 2026

ES Pro is a working tool. It gets used on a phone, outdoors, in a van, in the dark, with gloves on and one hand free. If it can't be read or heard, it can't be used. So this isn't a box we tick; it's the app doing its job.

Here's what works today, what doesn't yet, and how to tell us when we've got it wrong. All of it is on for everyone: there's no separate “accessible mode,” and nothing here costs extra.

What works

The accessibility features Apple lists on the App Store, and where ES Pro stands on each.
FeatureWhere it standsWhat that means in the app
VoiceOver Works Buttons, rows, and fields say what they are, in English and in French. A job reads as one sentence (what the job is, its state, when, which crew, and the client) instead of a dozen fragments you have to piece back together. A day on the month calendar tells you its date, how many jobs are on it, and the name of any holiday, because the coloured dots and lines can't be read out loud. Pop-up messages and the offline banner are read out as they appear, and with VoiceOver on, a message stays up for six seconds and gets a close button.
Voice Control Works Say “tap Save” and it taps Save. Voice Control uses the same names VoiceOver reads. The buttons at the top of each screen, Cancel and Save on every form, and the main actions on a job are full-size targets you can hit with gloves on. The few smaller ones are listed under “Where we fall short.”
Bigger text Works ES Pro follows your iPhone's Larger Text setting. It also has its own Text Size setting (Small, Medium, Large, Extra Large) for when you want this app bigger, or smaller, without changing every other app. The two multiply together, up to 2.2 times normal size. Screens rearrange to fit rather than cutting words off. Turn on Bold Text and the app's text gets heavier too.
Dark appearance Works Follows your iPhone until you decide for yourself with the Dark Mode switch in the app. Dark isn't light mode turned inside out; it has its own colours, drawn on purpose.
Not colour alone Mostly A job's state is a badge with a word on it (Scheduled, In Progress, Overdue, Complete, Cancelled), and Overdue adds a warning sign. Losing signal says “offline” in words. A client's type is an icon and a word. On the month calendar, the crew dots and the holiday line are read out as a number of jobs and the holiday's name. Two smaller calendar views still lean on colour; see “Where we fall short.”
Contrast Partly Main text and secondary text meet the usual web standard for contrast (WCAG AA) in both light and dark, so they stay readable in a bright driveway or a dim basement. Turn on Increase Contrast and secondary text, borders, and dividers get stronger still. Our lightest grey text doesn't meet that standard yet; see “Where we fall short.”
Reduce Motion Mostly Turn on Reduce Motion and screens stop sliding, calendar months stop swinging, the loading shimmer stops, charts appear without growing, and Back to top jumps instead of scrolling. Some things still move; see “Where we fall short.”
Captions Nothing to caption There's no video in ES Pro, and job photos are still pictures. The only sound is your iPhone's normal notification alert, and every notification is written out too.
Audio descriptions Nothing to describe Same story: no video anywhere in the app.

Settings inside ES Pro

Open Settings from the menu:

  • Dark Mode. A switch. Until you touch it, the app follows your iPhone; once you do, it stays the way you set it.
  • Text Size. Small, Medium, Large, or Extra Large, with a preview, and nothing changes until you tap Apply. It changes the whole app and multiplies with your iPhone's Larger Text. The preview shows the app's own setting on its own.
  • Language. English or French. Until you pick one, it matches your iPhone. The labels VoiceOver reads, notifications, the Lock Screen job card, the home-screen widget, and CarPlay all follow your choice. Siri is the exception: it answers in your iPhone's Siri language.
  • Notifications. A shortcut straight to your iPhone's notification settings for ES Pro, for when reminders stop arriving. If the app has never asked to send notifications, it asks first.
  • Replay app tour. Brings the tips back. They show again on each screen as you visit it, as many times as you like.

Settings on your iPhone

These live on the phone, not in ES Pro. Turn one on and the app picks it up straight away.

  • VoiceOver: Settings → Accessibility → VoiceOver
  • Voice Control: Settings → Accessibility → Voice Control
  • Larger Text: Settings → Accessibility → Display & Text Size → Larger Text
  • Bold Text: Settings → Accessibility → Display & Text Size → Bold Text
  • Increase Contrast: Settings → Accessibility → Display & Text Size → Increase Contrast
  • Reduce Motion: Settings → Accessibility → Motion → Reduce Motion
  • Dark appearance: Settings → Display & Brightness → Dark

Where we fall short

We'd rather tell you here than have you find out on a job at four in the afternoon.

  • The crew map. The map itself is Google's, and VoiceOver can't reach the individual pins on it. Use the Show staff list button on the map instead: you get the same people as a list, with each person's name, their town, and how long ago their location was updated. If you're sharing your own location, the list also shows how far away each person is, closest first. Tap a name to open that person's card, which has their full address.
  • The weekly chart. On the dashboard, “Completed vs cancelled” reads as two totals for the last eight weeks rather than week by week, and the weekly numbers aren't written out anywhere else. “Jobs booked per day” reads day by day.
  • The lightest grey text. Some small, light grey text falls below the contrast standard, and it's worst in light appearance: the weekday letters above the calendar, the time on a job card, the small labels above form fields, and the hint in search boxes. Increase Contrast doesn't darken it yet.
  • Colour on the smaller calendars. When the calendar is folded down to a single week, and in the date picker you book from, a holiday shows only as a coloured line and VoiceOver reads just the date. The week view's crew dots aren't counted out loud either. Open the full month to hear both.
  • Some things still move. With Reduce Motion on, pop-up messages still slide in, sheets still slide up, dialogs still fade in, photos still zoom open, and the text size and location sharing screens still slide in.
  • A few small targets. Most things you tap are full size, but a few aren't: the handle that shows or hides the calendar (full width, but short), the month arrows in the booking date picker, and the “Use client's address” and “Change” links on the booking form.
  • Status badges stop growing. Past about 1.3× they hold their size so “In Progress” stays on one line instead of breaking apart. Everything else keeps getting bigger.
  • Job photos have no description. VoiceOver can't tell you what's in a photo. They're snapped on site by whoever's there, and nobody stops to write a caption. The photo thumbnails on a job aren't announced as something you can open, either.
  • We haven't checked every corner. We test the paths people walk every day (signing in, reading the schedule, opening a job, marking it done) with VoiceOver on, Reduce Motion on, and the text turned up. Past that we're relying on you. If a screen doesn't work for you, that's a bug, not a feature request, and we'll treat it like one.

Tell us what's in your way

If any part of ES Pro is hard or impossible to use, write to george@vogas.net and put Accessibility in the subject. We answer within 2 business days.

It helps if you can say what you were trying to do, where you were in the app, what you use to get around (VoiceOver, bigger text, something else), and which iPhone and which version of iOS.

We'll tell you straight whether we can reproduce it, whether we're fixing it, and we'll come back to you when the fix ships.
